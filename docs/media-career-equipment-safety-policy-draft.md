# Media Career Program — Equipment & Safety Policy Draft

> Internal draft. Final liability wording requires legal review before learner-facing use.

## 1. Objective

Protect:

- people;
- equipment;
- client property;
- studio;
- production continuity;
- data.

The policy is not intended to discourage equipment use. Safe hands-on practice is part of the program.

## 2. Equipment access levels

Recommended:

### OBSERVE

Learner may observe but not independently operate the equipment.

### SUPERVISED

Learner may operate under mentor/crew supervision.

### CLEARED

Learner may operate specified equipment within approved training/production scope.

Clearance is equipment/category-specific.

## 3. Mandatory induction

Before equipment access:

- handling;
- setup/pack-down;
- battery/power;
- media/cards;
- cables;
- stands/grip;
- load limits;
- weather/moisture;
- transport;
- data handling;
- emergency stop/escalation.

## 4. Checkout record

Record:

- learner;
- date/time;
- project/activity;
- equipment;
- serial/asset ID where available;
- accessories;
- storage media;
- condition;
- approver;
- expected return.

## 5. Return record

Record:

- return time;
- missing accessories;
- damage;
- battery/card status;
- abnormal operation;
- incident number if applicable.

## 6. Prohibited actions

Without explicit approval, learner must not:

- lend equipment to another person;
- take equipment off-site;
- modify/repair equipment;
- mount equipment beyond rated load;
- use damaged power/cables;
- override safety procedures;
- leave high-value gear unattended;
- format cards before data handoff/backup is confirmed.

## 7. Camera/data safety

Before card formatting or data deletion:

- verify designated copy exists;
- verify required backup step;
- follow folder/file naming standard;
- confirm handoff owner.

“Thought it was backed up” is not sufficient evidence.

## 8. Grip / lighting safety

Core controls:

- stabilize stands;
- use counterweight/sandbags where required;
- keep walkways clear;
- manage cables;
- respect heat/weather/water risk;
- do not rig overhead equipment without qualified supervision;
- do not exceed load ratings;
- isolate unsafe equipment immediately.

## 9. Gimbal / handheld rigs

Check:

- total payload;
- battery/power routing;
- arm/mount security;
- clearance around people/property;
- operator fatigue;
- safe set-down location.

## 10. Electrical safety

Learners must not improvise mains-power repairs or unsafe electrical distribution.

Suspected fault:

```
Stop use
→ isolate
→ notify responsible crew/mentor
→ label/hold equipment
```

## 11. Incident classes

### NORMAL WEAR

Expected deterioration through proper use.

### ACCIDENT

Unintended event despite reasonable process.

### NEGLIGENCE

Failure to follow known/communicated process.

### SERIOUS MISCONDUCT

Intentional misuse, unauthorized removal, concealment, or equivalent serious conduct.

Financial liability must not be stated as automatic “100% replacement cost” without the approved legal policy.

## 12. Incident report

Record:

- date/time;
- people;
- equipment/property;
- activity;
- what happened;
- immediate action;
- injury/safety consequence;
- data/client impact;
- photos/evidence;
- next action;
- reviewer.

## 13. Stop-work authority

Any learner or team member should be able to stop an activity when they reasonably identify an immediate safety risk.

Stopping an unsafe setup is not failure to perform.

## 14. Travel / location work

Before off-site activity confirm:

- transport;
- equipment owner;
- weather;
- access;
- power;
- storage;
- learner supervision;
- emergency contact;
- working time;
- return/handoff.

## 15. Draft legal point

Before learner-facing publication, legal review must align equipment damage/compensation wording with the Enrollment Agreement and applicable law.
