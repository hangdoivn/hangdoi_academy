# Appendix H — Safety & Data SOP Draft

**DRAFT — PROGRAM / LEGAL / SAFETY REVIEW REQUIRED**

This SOP defines the minimum operational controls expected during practical Media training and approved production-learning activities.

It must be adapted to the actual facility, equipment inventory, trainer structure and applicable safety requirements before use.

## 1. Scope

Applies to:

- classroom practicals;
- studio exercises;
- lighting/grip practice;
- camera/audio/gimbal practice;
- post-production labs;
- capstone;
- approved real-client production exposure.

## 2. Roles

### Program Director

Responsible for:

- approving program-wide safety/data rules;
- assigning competent supervisors;
- incident escalation;
- periodic SOP review.

### Trainer / Mentor

Responsible for:

- pre-task briefing;
- equipment/task supervision;
- stopping unsafe activity;
- documenting incidents and material breaches.

### Learner

Responsible for:

- following instructions;
- checking equipment before use;
- reporting faults/incidents;
- protecting cards/files/client data;
- not bypassing safety/data controls.

## 3. General safety rule

Any trainer/supervisor may stop an activity immediately where there is a material risk to:

- people;
- property;
- equipment;
- client/location;
- data;
- confidential material.

Stopping a task for safety is not treated as learner failure by itself.

## 4. Camera / lens handling

Before use:

- check body/lens condition;
- confirm strap/cage/plate where required;
- verify mount lock;
- check battery/card;
- inspect for loose accessories.

During use:

- do not leave unsecured cameras on unstable surfaces;
- use safe handover;
- do not change lenses in high-risk dust/water conditions unless instructed;
- report impact/drop immediately.

## 5. Lighting / electrical

Minimum rules:

- inspect cables/plugs before use;
- keep cable paths controlled;
- avoid overloaded outlets;
- use approved power distribution;
- do not handle unsafe electrical equipment;
- allow hot fixtures to cool before handling;
- secure stands;
- use weights/sandbags where required;
- maintain clearance from flammable material.

Only authorized persons may perform higher-risk electrical setup.

## 6. Grip / stands / overhead equipment

Rules:

- secure stands before mounting equipment;
- verify load limits;
- tighten locks;
- use safety cables where required;
- keep weighted legs oriented correctly;
- establish no-go zones below overhead gear;
- never leave elevated equipment unattended where unsafe.

## 7. Gimbal / movement

Rules:

- balance before power-on where required;
- maintain clear movement path;
- use spotter where visibility is restricted;
- do not walk backward blindly;
- use extra control on stairs/uneven ground;
- stop if balance/mechanical fault occurs.

## 8. Audio / cables

Rules:

- route cables to minimize trip risk;
- secure runs across walkways;
- monitor levels at safe listening volume;
- sanitize shared close-contact equipment where applicable.

## 9. Lifting / moving equipment

Rules:

- use two-person lift for heavy/awkward loads;
- use carts/cases where available;
- do not rush on stairs;
- communicate before moving stands/fixtures;
- trainer may restrict learner lifting based on task risk.

## 10. Location / client premises

Before work:

- receive location/client rules;
- identify restricted zones;
- identify emergency exits;
- confirm permitted shooting areas;
- protect public/customer privacy;
- keep gear from blocking operations.

Learners must not represent themselves as authorized client representatives.

## 11. Travel / night activity

Where applicable:

- confirm transport plan;
- define supervisor/contact;
- avoid learner isolation;
- record start/end;
- define safe return arrangement;
- review fatigue risk.

Any mandatory participation outside normal schedule must be approved under the final program rules.

## 12. Food / hospitality environments

When working around food/service operations:

- follow hygiene instructions;
- avoid contaminating food-contact surfaces;
- keep production equipment out of unsafe service paths;
- comply with kitchen/venue restrictions.

## 13. Incident reporting

Record at minimum:

- date/time;
- location;
- people involved;
- activity;
- what occurred;
- immediate action;
- injury/damage/data impact;
- witness/supervisor;
- follow-up;
- closure.

## 14. Equipment fault / damage

If equipment appears unsafe or damaged:

1. stop using it;
2. isolate/tag where possible;
3. inform supervisor;
4. record asset/issue;
5. do not return to service until approved.

Learner financial liability must not be assumed automatically.

## 15. Data integrity — media handling

Minimum workflow:

1. identify source media/card;
2. copy using approved workflow;
3. verify transfer;
4. create required backup;
5. maintain naming/folder structure;
6. do not format source media until authorized;
7. record handover where required.

## 16. Card / storage control

Rules:

- separate used/unused cards;
- do not reuse cards before backup authorization;
- avoid personal storage for client-confidential media unless approved;
- label drives/cards consistently;
- report missing media immediately.

## 19. Client/confidential data

Learners may only access the minimum required data.

Prohibited unless approved:

- copying raw client files to personal storage;
- uploading to personal cloud;
- sharing private links;
- posting BTS/unreleased content;
- retaining confidential files after required deletion.

## 20. Credentials

Rules:

- do not share passwords in chat/documents;
- do not store credentials in project files;
- use delegated access where available;
- revoke temporary access after activity;
- report suspected compromise immediately.

## 21. Data incident

Examples:

- lost card/drive;
- unauthorized upload/share;
- account compromise;
- accidental deletion;
- confidential client leak.

Immediate response:

1. stop further exposure;
2. notify Program Director/data owner;
3. preserve evidence/logs;
4. identify affected project/data;
5. follow approved incident/privacy procedure.

## 22. Hard-gate assessment

A learner must not be marked Production Ready with unresolved material failure in:

- safety;
- data integrity;
- serious professional conduct.

Retest/rework may be allowed where appropriate and safe.

## 23. Briefing record

For higher-risk practicals record:

- activity;
- date;
- trainer;
- learners;
- safety topics;
- data rules;
- questions/issues.
